Street-Style Beef Tacos

Saucy homemade-seasoned ground beef in warm, oil-kissed corn tortillas, finished taco-stand style with onion, cilantro, cotija, and lime.

Ingredients Steps
4 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 4 servings

Prep: 15 minutes

Cook: 15 minutes

Total: 30 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef (90/10 or 93/7)
  • 1 Tbsp avocado oil or beef tallow
  • 1 small onion, finely diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 Tbsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp dried oregano, crushed in your palm
  • 1/2 tsp onion powder
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp cornstarch
  • 1/2 cup beef broth (or tomato sauce for a richer, sweeter taco)
  • 12 white or yellow corn tortillas
  • 2 Tbsp vegetable oil, for warming the tortillas
  • 1/2 white onion, finely diced, for garnish
  • 1 bunch fresh cilantro, chopped
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • Crumbled cotija cheese, for topping

Instructions

  1. Stir together the chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, oregano, onion powder, salt, black pepper, and cornstarch in a small bowl. Toss the white onion and cilantro together in another bowl for the garnish.
  2. Heat the avocado oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Add the ground beef and sear, breaking it up, until browned, about 6 minutes.
  3. Add the small onion and garlic and cook, stirring, until soft, about 3 minutes.
  4. Sprinkle in the spice blend and toast it with the beef for 1 minute.
  5. Pour in the beef broth and simmer, stirring, until the sauce is thick and glossy, about 3 minutes. Keep warm over low heat.
  6. Heat a second skillet or griddle over medium heat with about 1 tsp of the vegetable oil. Warm each tortilla in the oil, about 15 seconds per side, until hot, soft, and lightly blistered but not crisp. Stack them in a towel and add oil as needed.
  7. Spoon the beef down the center of each tortilla (double them up if they are thin). Top with the onion-cilantro mix and cotija, and squeeze lime over each taco right before eating.

Notes: Toasting the cumin in a dry pan before mixing deepens the flavor if you have a spare minute. Fattier 80/20 beef works too; just spoon off some of the fat before adding the spices.
