Savannah Red Rice with Crispy Chicken Thighs

Classic Lowcountry red rice baked with bacon, the trinity, and tomatoes, topped with crackly oven-roasted skin-on chicken thighs. A double batch, so Wednesday is leftovers.

Ingredients Steps
8 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 8 servings

Prep: 20 minutes

Cook: 55 minutes

Total: 1 hour 15 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 4 lb bone-in skin-on chicken thighs (about 10)
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 8 slices thick-cut bacon, chopped
  • 1 large onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 2 stalks celery, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 (6 oz) can tomato paste
  • 1 (28 oz) can crushed tomatoes
  • 2 cups chicken bone broth
  • 3 cups long-grain white rice
  • 1 tsp Cajun seasoning
  • 2 tsp sugar
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 4 green onions, sliced

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F. Season the chicken thighs with 1 tsp of the salt, the smoked paprika, garlic powder, and black pepper.
  2. Cook the bacon in a large heavy oven-safe pot with a lid over medium heat until crisp, 7 minutes. Add the onion, bell pepper, and celery and cook 5 minutes, then the garlic and tomato paste for 2 minutes.
  3. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, chicken bone broth, rice, Cajun seasoning, sugar, cayenne, and the rest of the salt and bring to a simmer, 3 minutes.
  4. Cover tightly and bake on the lower rack 50 minutes without stirring. At the same time, put the chicken thighs skin side up on a rack set in a sheet pan on the upper rack and roast 50 minutes.
  5. Take the rice out and leave it covered. Raise the oven to 450°F and roast the chicken 8 more minutes, until the skin is crackly and the chicken reads 185°F.
  6. Fluff the rice, top with the chicken and green onions, and serve.

Notes: This is a double batch: save half for Wednesday's leftovers. Reheat the chicken on a sheet pan at 400°F for 15 minutes to re-crisp the skin, and the rice covered with a splash of broth. Red rice is traditionally baked so the grains stay separate; do not lift the lid early.
