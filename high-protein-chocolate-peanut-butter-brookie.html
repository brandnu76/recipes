High-Protein Chocolate Peanut Butter Brookie

A 10-inch skillet split down the middle: peanut-butter cookie on one side, cocoa brownie on the other. Cottage cheese, peanut butter powder, and one scoop of chocolate protein. About 17 grams of protein per square.

Ingredients Steps
8 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 8 squares

Prep: 15 minutes

Cook: 20 minutes

Total: 35 minutes, plus 15 minutes rest

Ingredients

Peanut butter cookie side

  • 1/2 cup low-fat cottage cheese (113 g)
  • 3 tablespoons natural peanut butter (48 g)
  • 1/4 cup peanut butter powder (24 g)
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 large egg white
  • 2 tablespoons packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup oat flour (20 g)
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • Pinch of fine salt

Chocolate brownie side

  • 1/2 cup low-fat cottage cheese (113 g)
  • 1/2 cup nonfat plain Greek yogurt (120 g)
  • 1 scoop chocolate whey or casein protein powder (about 30 g)
  • 1 large egg
  • 3 tablespoons unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 tablespoon packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup oat flour (20 g)
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 teaspoon baking soda
  • Pinch of fine salt
  • 3 tablespoons mini chocolate chips, divided
  • Flaky salt, for the top
  • Butter or oil, for the skillet

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Butter a 10-inch cast-iron skillet, or line an 8-inch square pan with parchment. Cottage-cheese batter sticks if the pan is bare.
  2. Blend the peanut-butter batter until smooth: cottage cheese, peanut butter, peanut butter powder, egg, egg white, brown sugar, vanilla, oat flour, baking soda, and salt. Scrape the jar and blend again if curds remain. A whisk works if you start with blended cottage cheese.
  3. Rinse the blender. Blend the brownie batter: cottage cheese, Greek yogurt, protein powder, egg, cocoa, brown sugar, oat flour, vanilla, baking soda, and salt. Stir in 2 tablespoons of the chocolate chips by hand.
  4. Spread the peanut-butter batter on one half of the skillet and the brownie batter on the other. Drag a knife once through the seam. Scatter the last tablespoon of chips over both sides and finish with flaky salt.
  5. Bake 18 to 22 minutes. The edges should be set and the center should still look slightly soft. A toothpick at the edge comes out with moist crumbs, not raw batter. Pull it before the center dries out.
  6. Rest 15 minutes in the pan. Cut 8 squares. The seam is the point. Each piece should carry both batters.

Notes

No coffee and no espresso powder. Cocoa and peanut butter carry the flavor.

One scoop of chocolate protein is in the brownie side on purpose. A sweetened scoop means you can drop the brownie-side brown sugar. Unflavored protein works; add the sugar back. Skip the powder and add another 1/2 cup cottage cheese if you want it out. The crumb is denser and each square loses about 3 grams of protein.

Peanut butter powder is the fat cut. The 3 tablespoons of real peanut butter are there so it still tastes like peanut butter, not a shake. Do not swap all of it for jar peanut butter or the skillet turns greasy and the protein density drops.

Oat flour is rolled oats blitzed in a blender. All-purpose flour, same weight, works.

Estimated from USDA values for 1% cottage cheese, nonfat Greek yogurt, natural peanut butter, powdered peanut butter, eggs, cocoa, oat flour, brown sugar, and a 30 g chocolate whey scoop at 24 g protein, plus mini chips: about 200 calories, 17 g protein, 16 g carbohydrate, and 8 g fat per square (8 squares). Brands move those numbers. Cut 6 squares and each lands near 23 grams of protein and 265 calories.
