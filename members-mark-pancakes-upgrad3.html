Member's Mark Pancakes Upgrad3

Member's Mark buttermilk pancake mix finished with whole milk, an egg, melted butter, and vanilla. Rest the batter, then cook it on a buttered griddle until the edges set. Richer and fluffier than the bag directions, and still a weekday stack.

Ingredients Steps Notes
4 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 4 servings (about 8 pancakes)

Prep: 5 minutes

Cook: 15 minutes

Total: about 25 minutes, including the rest

Ingredients

  • 1 cup Member's Mark Buttermilk Pancake Mix
  • 3/4 cup whole milk (or 1/2 cup milk plus 1/4 cup buttermilk) — start here; adjust for desired thickness
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons melted butter, cooled slightly
  • 1/2 to 1 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons club soda or seltzer for extra lightness, or a pinch of cinnamon, optional

Instructions

  1. Whisk the milk, egg, melted butter, and vanilla together until combined.
  2. Add the dry mix and stir gently with a fork or spatula just until the major dry pockets disappear. Lumps are fine and desirable.
  3. Let the batter rest 5 to 10 minutes while the griddle heats. It will thicken slightly.
  4. Heat a griddle or heavy skillet over medium heat. Test with a drop of water — it should sizzle and dance, not instantly vanish. Brush or wipe with butter, or butter plus a neutral oil.
  5. Pour 1/4-cup portions. Cook until bubbles form across the surface and the edges look set, about 2 to 3 minutes. Flip once and cook until golden.

Notes

This approach consistently turns the bulk bag into noticeably better pancakes — richer, fluffier, more flavorful — while still keeping the convenience that made you buy the 10-lb bag in the first place.

For thicker, taller pancakes, use slightly less liquid. For thinner, more spreadable ones, add a tablespoon or two more milk.

Freeze extras in a single layer, then bag them. Reheat in the toaster for quick weekday stacks.

The mix already contains buttermilk powder and egg solids, so these additions simply restore the moisture, fat, and fresh structure that powdered versions lose.
