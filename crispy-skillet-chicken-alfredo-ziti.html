Crispy Skillet Chicken Alfredo with Ziti

Bone-in, skin-on thighs seared hard in cast iron and finished in a hot oven, then the same skillet becomes a silky garlic-Parmesan Alfredo for a pound of ziti. Crackly skin on top, rich sauce in every tube.

Ingredients Steps
6 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 6 servings

Prep: 15 minutes

Cook: 45 minutes

Total: 1 hour

Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

Chicken

  • 6 to 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 3 lb)
  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 Tbsp olive oil or beef tallow
  • 1 Tbsp butter, optional for basting

Pasta

  • 1 lb ziti
  • Kosher salt for the pasta water

Alfredo sauce

  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • 3/4 tsp garlic powder
  • 3/4 tsp Italian seasoning
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 2 cups freshly grated Parmesan, plus more to serve
  • Chopped fresh parsley, to serve

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F with a rack in the middle.
  2. Pat the chicken thighs very dry with paper towels, then season both sides with the Italian seasoning, kosher salt, and black pepper. If you have time, dry brine them uncovered on a rack in the fridge for a few hours or overnight; the skin dries out and crisps even harder.
  3. Set a 12-inch cast-iron skillet over medium heat with a thin film of the oil or tallow. Lay the thighs in skin side down and leave them alone for 10 minutes, until the fat renders and the skin is deep golden and releases on its own.
  4. Flip the thighs skin side up, drop in the optional butter and spoon it over, then slide the whole skillet into the oven. Roast 20 minutes, until the thickest thigh reads 180 to 185°F. Thighs are juicier well past 165, so trust the higher number.
  5. While the chicken roasts, bring a big pot of well-salted water to a boil and cook the ziti until just al dente, following the box (usually twelve to fourteen), timed to land with the sauce. Save 1/2 cup of the pasta water before you drain.
  6. Move the thighs to a board skin side up and leave them uncovered so the skin stays crisp. Rest them 5 minutes. Use a mitt, because that skillet handle is 425°F. Pour off all but about 1 Tbsp of fat and keep the browned bits.
  7. Set the skillet over medium-low. Melt the butter into the heavy cream, scraping up the fond, then whisk in the minced garlic, garlic powder, Italian seasoning, salt, and black pepper. Hold it at a gentle simmer 3 to 4 minutes; do not let it boil.
  8. Take the skillet off the heat and stir in the Parmesan a handful at a time until smooth. Toss in the drained ziti, loosening with splashes of the pasta water until glossy, 2 minutes.
  9. Cut the chicken off the bone and lay it over the pasta with the crispy skin on top. Serve right away with extra Parmesan, cracked black pepper, and parsley.

Notes: Why ziti: the tubes trap the sauce, so every bite carries Alfredo. Penne, rigatoni, or classic fettuccine all work the same way. Why bone-in, skin-on: more flavor, more collagen, and fat you cook with instead of buying. Save the bones in the freezer for stock. Timing: the chicken takes about 30 minutes start to finish, so drop the pasta as the thighs go into the oven. Add this recipe to Plan a Meal with a salad and it builds the timeline for you. Adapted from a weeknight chicken Alfredo at bellyfull.net, reworked for bone-in thighs, cast iron, and ziti.
