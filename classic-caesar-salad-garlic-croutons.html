Classic Caesar Salad with Garlic Parmesan Croutons

Cold, crunchy romaine in a punchy garlic-lemon-Dijon dressing with shaved Parmesan and golden garlic-Parmesan baguette croutons baked on the bias.

Ingredients Steps
4 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 4 servings

Prep: 20 minutes

Cook: 12 minutes

Total: 32 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

Croutons

  • 1/2 French baguette, sliced 1/4 inch thick on the bias
  • 3 Tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1 tsp minced garlic
  • 2 Tbsp grated Parmesan

Dressing

  • 2 small garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 tsp lemon juice
  • 1 1/2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 1/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1/2 tsp sea salt
  • 1/8 tsp black pepper
  • 1 to 2 anchovy fillets, minced, optional

Salad

  • 1 large head romaine (or 2 small), washed, dried, and torn
  • 1/3 cup shaved Parmesan

Instructions

  1. Wash the romaine, spin it very dry, and tear it into bite-size pieces. Keep it chilled in the fridge.
  2. Dressing: In a bowl, whisk the garlic, Dijon, Worcestershire, lemon juice, red wine vinegar, sea salt, black pepper, and the anchovy if using. Drizzle in the olive oil while whisking until creamy. Refrigerate up to 3 days.
  3. Croutons: Heat the oven to 350°F. Stir the olive oil and minced garlic together, brush or rub it over the baguette slices, and spread them on a sheet pan. Sprinkle with the grated Parmesan and bake 10 to 12 minutes, until golden and crisp. Cool on the pan; they crunch up more as they cool. Make them up to a day ahead and store airtight.
  4. Just before serving, toss the romaine with the dressing until every leaf is coated, then top with the shaved Parmesan and croutons. Serve immediately.

Notes: Make ahead: the dressing keeps up to 3 days in the fridge and the croutons stay crisp a day in an airtight container, so Plan a Meal puts both in Do earlier. For classic depth, mash one or two anchovy fillets into the dressing; nobody will name it, but they will notice. Dry greens matter: wet leaves water down the dressing. Inspired by the Caesar salad at Natasha's Kitchen, written our way.
