Pellet-Grill Reverse-Sear Tri-Tip

Tri-tip smoked low at 225°F and seared hard in cast iron, with loaded baked potatoes and garlicky charred broccolini.

Ingredients Steps
4 servings

By Brandon Carroll

Yield: 4 servings

Prep: 15 minutes

Cook: 1 hour 30 minutes

Total: 1 hour 45 minutes

Difficulty: Easy

Ingredients

  • 1 tri-tip roast, about 2 1/2 lb
  • 2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1 Tbsp avocado oil, for the tri-tip
  • 4 medium russet potatoes, scrubbed
  • 1 Tbsp avocado oil, for the potatoes
  • 2 Tbsp unsalted butter
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1/2 cup shredded cheddar
  • 2 Tbsp chopped chives
  • 1 lb broccolini, trimmed
  • 1 Tbsp avocado oil, for the broccolini
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, for the broccolini
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper, for the broccolini
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced

Instructions

  1. Heat the pellet grill to 225°F. Pat the tri-tip dry, rub it with the avocado oil for the tri-tip, and season with the kosher salt, black pepper, garlic powder, and smoked paprika.
  2. Rub the potatoes with the avocado oil for the potatoes and a pinch of salt, and set them on the grill away from the hottest spot.
  3. Lay the tri-tip fat side up on the grill next to the potatoes. Smoke 60 minutes, until the center reads 120°F and a fork slides easily into the potatoes.
  4. Toss the broccolini with the avocado oil for the broccolini, the salt and pepper for the broccolini, and the garlic. Char it in a very hot cast-iron skillet, turning once, about 5 minutes. Move it to a plate.
  5. Sear the tri-tip in the same ripping-hot skillet 2 minutes per side, until the center hits 130°F for medium-rare. Rest it 10 minutes.
  6. Split the potatoes, fluff the centers, and top with the butter, a pinch of salt, the cheddar, sour cream, and chives. Slice the tri-tip against the grain (the grain changes direction where the two muscles meet) and serve with the broccolini.

Notes: Thickness matters: a thin tri-tip may hit 120°F in 45 minutes and a thick one can take 75, so go by the thermometer. If the potatoes need more time, finish them in a 425°F oven.
